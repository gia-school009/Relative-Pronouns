GRAMMAR SPOT

1

We use which, that, who and where to join sentences when the second sentence gives us important information.

a We bought a house. It cost £300,000.

We bought a house which/that cost £300,000.

b I met a man. He was from my hometown.

I met a man who was from my hometown.

c The hotel was wonderful. We stayed there.

The hotel where we stayed was wonderful.

In-Depth: Understanding Relative Pronouns

Relative pronouns connect two clauses together, making our speaking and writing more fluid by avoiding repetition.

Who

Used for people. It replaces subject pronouns like he, she, or they.

I know a girl. She speaks French.

I know a girl who speaks French.

Which / That

Used for things or animals. In defining clauses, they are usually interchangeable.

This is the book. It won an award.

This is the book that won an award.

Where

Used for places. It replaces location words like there or in that place.

That's the park. We met there.

That's the park where we met.

Common Mistake!

Do not repeat the pronoun (he, she, it, they, there) in the second half of the sentence after using a relative pronoun.

  • Incorrect: The man who he called you is my uncle.
  • Correct: The man who called you is my uncle.
